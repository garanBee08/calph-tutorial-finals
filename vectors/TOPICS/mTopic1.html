Physical Quantities

A physical quantity is the property of a material or system that can be measured or quantified. This means that a physical quantity can be expressed as the combination of a numerical value and a unit, creating a standard for scientific and technical communication.

Principles

Dimensions
It's the concept of understanding what a form of measure and what it tells supported to mathematically represent.
Unit
  • A unit is a specially chosen amount of a physical quantity used to measure and express physical quantities.
  • The SI unit or Système International d'Unités 'French' is the current System of Units. It has seven common and widely accepted values of measurement which include length, mass, time, electric current, thermodynamic temperature, amount of substance, and luminous intensity.

Support

Scalar
A scalar quantity is a physical quantity that has magnitude but has no direction.
Vectors
A vector quantity is a physical quantity that has magnitude AND direction, and that obeys the triangle or parallelogram law of vector addition (is said to be a vector only account of measurement.)

International System of Quantities (Base Quantities)

The SI defines seven base quantities, which are the fundamental building blocks from which all other quantities are derived:

  • Length (Unit: meter, m)
  • Mass (Unit: kilogram, kg)
  • Time (Unit: second, s)
  • Electric Current (Unit: ampere, A)
  • Temperature (Unit: kelvin, K)
  • Amount of Substance (Unit: mole, mol)
  • Luminous Intensity (Unit: candela, cd)
2. General Derived Quantities

International System of Quantities (Base Quantities)

The SI defines seven base quantities, which are the fundamental building blocks from which all other quantities are derived:

  • Area (Unit: m²)
  • Volume (Unit: m³)
  • Velocity (Unit: m/s)
  • Acceleration (Unit: m/s²)
  • Force (Unit: Newton - N)
  • Energy (Unit: Joule - J)
  • Power (Unit: Watt - W)
  • Frequency (Unit: Hertz - Hz)
  • Pressure (Unit: Pascal - Pa)

Conclusion
