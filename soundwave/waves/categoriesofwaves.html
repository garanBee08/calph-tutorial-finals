CATEGORIES OF
WAVES

Waves can be found everywhere — in water, sound, light, and even in the invisible signals that let us use the internet and phones. However, not all waves are the same. They can be grouped into different categories based on how they travel, what they travel through, and how particles in the medium move.

Mechanical Waves

Definition

Mechanical waves require a medium such as air, water, or solid material to travel through. They transfer energy by the vibration of particles in the medium.

Examples

Sound waves, water waves, and seismic waves are common examples of mechanical waves.

Electromagnetic Waves

Definition

Electromagnetic waves do not require a medium and can travel through a vacuum. They consist of oscillating electric and magnetic fields.

Examples

Light waves, radio waves, microwaves, X-rays, and gamma rays are examples of electromagnetic waves.

Pulse & Periodic Waves

Definition

Pulse waves are single disturbances moving through a medium, while periodic waves are continuous and repeat at regular intervals. Example: a single wave on a rope.

Periodic Wave

A periodic wave is created when a vibration or disturbance repeats at regular intervals. It has a continuous, repeating pattern and can be either transverse or longitudinal, depending on how the particles move. Periodic: sound waves, water waves, or vibrations of a guitar string.
