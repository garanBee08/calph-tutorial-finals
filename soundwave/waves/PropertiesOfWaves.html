PROPERTIES OF WAVE

Waves have characteristics that describe how they move and transfer energy. These include wavelength, frequency, amplitude, and speed, which help us understand their behavior in different mediums.

A

Amplitude (A)

The maximum displacement from equilibrium. A larger amplitude means higher energy.

λ

Wavelength (λ)

The distance between two identical points on consecutive cycles (e.g., crest to crest).

f

Frequency (f)

The number of oscillations or cycles per second, measured in hertz (Hz).

T

Period (T)

The time it takes for one full cycle to occur; related by f = 1/T.

v

Velocity (v)

Wave speed depends on the medium — for instance, sound moves faster in solids than in gases.

Rest

Rest Position

The undisturbed position of the medium when there’s no wave passing through.

Crest

Crest

The highest point of the wave above the rest position (maximum positive displacement).

Trough

Trough

The lowest point of the wave below the rest position (maximum negative displacement).
